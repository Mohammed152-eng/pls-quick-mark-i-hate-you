What 8,617 real attempts reveal about Cambridge MCQs

We sit on something most revision sites don't: real performance data from students doing actual past papers. Here's what it shows.
By Ved ShivaneÂ·Data as of 28 May 2026

Most advice about Cambridge past papers is guesswork. Ours isn't. Between January 2026 and May 2026, 801 students completed 8,617 timed multiple-choice attempts on QuickMark, answering 330,726 questions across 20 subjects. This page summarises what that anonymised data tells us about how hard each paper really is, the kind of thing you can't get from a mark scheme alone.

Every row below is an aggregate across all students who attempted that subject (minimum 30 attempts). The gap between the average and the median matters: where the median sits above the mean, it means most students score well and a smaller group of low scores drags the average down.

Three things the data makes obvious

Biology is the gentlest of the big IGCSE sciences, and the fastest. IGCSE Biology (0610) averages 87.4% and students finish in around 16 minutes, while IGCSE Physics (0625) is the toughest of the four at 80.4% and takes the longest at about 24.2 minutes. If you study all three sciences, that's a real clue about where your revision hours should go.

The jump from IGCSE to A Level is brutal, and measurable.The same subjects that sit in the mid-80s at IGCSE collapse at A Level: A Level Chemistry (9701) averages just 65.5% and A Level Physics (9702) 69.8%, versus 85.0% and 80.4% for their IGCSE versions. If you're stepping up, don't assume your IGCSE comfort carries over.

Most students cluster near the top, so the hard papers are where marks are won and lost. Because so many attempts land in the 80-100% band, the handful of genuinely difficult papers are disproportionately important. We break those down per subject below.
